Source Audit: The $8.30 Poverty-Line Subsection

A sentence-level check of source fit, factual precision, and the rial-conversion trail used in Poverty Dynamics in Iran.

Audit date: 10 July 2026 Branch: codex/audit-poverty-line-sources Scope: the newer Section 4.1 comparison subsection only

Executive Verdict

The benchmark itself is well documented. The local-currency implementation is arithmetically reproducible but is not yet validated as a World Bank poverty conversion.

$8.30 is supported

The World Bank reports $8.30 in 2021 PPP dollars as the line typical of upper-middle-income countries.

Rial conversion is provisional

The workbook uses a GDP PPP series. World Bank methodology prefers a household-final-consumption PPP for poverty.

Workbook source is unknown

Its early values match the current WDI GDP PPP series exactly, but the file contains no source, vintage, or retrieval date.

Poverty boundary corrected

The Stata code classifies equality with the threshold as poor. The revised text now says “less than or equal to.”

Publication consequence. The six rial values can remain in a draft only if they are labeled preliminary. Before final results are reported, the conversion should be reconstructed with documented poverty PPP, CPI, calendar, and day-to-month inputs, then every dependent estimate should be regenerated.

Revised Subsection

This is the substantive content now in paper_text/thesis.tex. Superscripts link to the evidence records below; author decisions and code-derived statements are identified in the sentence map.

The $8.30 per day threshold, measured in 2021 Purchasing Power Parity (PPP), is the World Bank's international poverty line for assessing poverty in upper-middle-income countries.E1, E2 It is derived from harmonized national poverty lines expressed in 2021 PPP dollars: the median for upper-middle-income countries is $8.29 and is rounded to $8.30.E1 This line is part of a tiered measurement system that also includes an extreme poverty line ($3.00) and a lower-middle-income line ($4.20), allowing poverty to be assessed against standards that are more relevant to countries at different income levels.E1, E2

The use of the 2021 PPP update, including the $8.30 line, is methodologically justified for several reasons. First, it incorporates the most recent ICP benchmark and updated national poverty lines used in global poverty monitoring.E1 Second, it ensures cross-country consistency by expressing household welfare in a common unit that accounts for differences in national price levels.E2 Third, the $8.30 line provides a more relevant benchmark for upper-middle-income countries, where the extreme poverty line is less informative.E2 Finally, it is consistent with the World Bank's current global poverty reporting framework, while estimates based on 2017 PPPs remain available through the Poverty and Inequality Platform.E2, E4

Nonetheless, PPP-based measures have recognized limitations. Changes in PPP benchmarks and in the national poverty lines underlying the global thresholds can revise measured poverty levels and historical series.E1, E2 International poverty lines also do not replace national poverty lines, which are more appropriate for country-specific assessment, policy dialogue, and targeting.E2 Consequently, the $8.30 (2021 PPP) line is used in this study as an international benchmark rather than as an official Iranian poverty line.

Required Before Final Results

The next methodological task is not to choose a nicer-looking conversion. It is to reconstruct the line from authoritative inputs and preserve the full calculation trail.

  1. Identify the poverty PPP.
    Obtain the 2021 household-final-consumption PPP for Iran actually used by the relevant PIP vintage.
  2. Fix the temporal index.
    Document the national CPI series, base period, Persian/Gregorian mapping, and any PIP exceptions.
  3. Fix the time unit.
    Record whether the monthly conversion uses 30 days, 365/12, or survey-specific month length.
  4. Regenerate and test.
    Rebuild all thresholds and dependent tables, then report sensitivity to a national or alternative international line.
